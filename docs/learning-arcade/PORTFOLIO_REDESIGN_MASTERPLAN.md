# SukuuNova Learning Arcade — Portfolio Redesign Masterplan

Status: **Pre-production portfolio map**  
Scope: all 18 live flagships plus future incubation worlds  

## Portfolio strategy

The goal is not to maximize the number of icons in the Arcade. The goal is to create a small set of deep worlds that can support multiple age bands, curriculum strands and years of meaningful progression.

Every current flagship is therefore classified as one of:

- **Keep & deepen** — core fantasy already supports a real game.
- **Radically redesign** — identity is useful, implementation is too quiz-like.
- **Expand into a world** — strong idea should become a longer career/adventure ecosystem.
- **Reframe** — preserve useful content but change genre/fantasy.
- **Incubate** — future concept, not immediate production.

# Current 18 flagships

## 1. Nova Runner — Mathematics Adventure Runner

**Decision:** Keep & deepen.

**Fantasy:** You are a courier/explorer crossing unstable mathematical worlds to restore Nova pathways.

**Core verbs:** run, jump, slide, choose routes, collect/use mathematical power-ups, manipulate gates.

**Learning-action:** number sense, arithmetic, fractions, geometry and later algebra determine route choice, jump timing, power-up combination and hazard solutions.

**Primary:** action-adventure with visual quantities and route puzzles.

**JHS:** speed/trajectory, ratio, algebraic gates, coordinate routes and optimization.

**Distinct UX:** full-screen motion game, minimal HUD, world map between runs. Never a centred question card.

**Progression:** biomes, movement abilities, route mastery, challenge contracts, ghost replays, character upgrades.

**Technology direction:** Phaser-class 2D game loop; optional lightweight physics.

## 2. TurboType — Typing League

**Decision:** Keep & deepen.

**Fantasy:** Compete in a futuristic typing sports league.

**Core verbs:** type, correct, maintain rhythm, manage accuracy, complete skill drills and races.

**Learning-action:** typing itself is the mechanic, so timing legitimately matters.

**Primary:** short races, letter/word mastery and posture/keyboard familiarity.

**JHS/SHS:** code-like text, professional typing, transcription, weak-key drills, accuracy tournaments.

**Distinct UX:** race track/tournament presentation, live keyboard heatmap, rhythm lane, competitor ghosts.

**Progression:** leagues, typing licences, personal-best ghosts, weak-key mastery, tournaments.

## 3. AstroLab — Physics Mission Control

**Decision:** Radically redesign.

**Fantasy:** Apprentice mission engineer helping operate spacecraft, rovers and orbital laboratories.

**Core verbs:** set force vectors, tune thrust, place masses, adjust angles, inspect telemetry, simulate, revise.

**Learning-action:** physics knowledge changes the actual simulated result.

**Primary:** visual cause-and-effect experiments with launch toys and simple forces.

**JHS:** motion, force, energy, circuits and measurement missions.

**SHS:** kinematics, vectors, orbital reasoning, energy systems and experiment design.

**Distinct UX:** mission-control panels plus interactive simulation viewport; no answer grid as primary input.

**Progression:** training lab → lunar missions → satellite rescue → deep-space programme.

**Technology direction:** 2D/3D simulation selected per mission; physics only where pedagogically meaningful.

## 4. Circuit Forge — Engineering Workshop

**Decision:** Radically redesign.

**Fantasy:** Build and repair power systems for a growing community/workshop.

**Core verbs:** place components, wire nodes, switch power, probe voltage/current, diagnose faults, replace parts.

**Learning-action:** circuit understanding is expressed through the circuit built and measurements taken.

**Primary:** batteries, bulbs, switches and safe paths.

**SHS:** deeper electrical calculations, load planning, sensors/control and engineering briefs.

**Distinct UX:** workbench + schematic + instruments. Dragging real components is the primary interaction.

**Progression:** home circuits → workshop → school/community microgrid → industrial control systems.

## 7. Reading Quest — Investigation Adventure

**Fantasy:** Become an explorer/reporter/detective who solves mysteries by reading evidence.

**Core verbs:** inspect passages/documents, highlight evidence, connect clues, annotate, compare sources, make inference boards.

**Learning-action:** comprehension is demonstrated through evidence collection and relationships.

# Shared world strategy

Long term, titles may share a universe without sharing gameplay.

Example:

- Cedi City is a city map containing businesses and jobs.
- CodeBots can have a robotics company physically located in the city.
- Signal Shield can protect city organizations.
- EcoGrid can manage city infrastructure.
- Style Studio can run a fashion business in the same world.

Cross-world connections should provide identity and continuity while preserving each game's controls and mechanics.

# Production order recommendation

## Wave 0 — Pre-production

1. Constitution, age architecture and Game Bible template.
2. Audit all existing 18 implementations against them.
3. Select one **vertical-slice proof title** and one **early-years proof title**.

Recommended proof titles:

- **Cedi City** — demonstrates deep simulation, Ghanaian context, career progression and mathematics/business integration.
- **Number Bloom** — demonstrates that early-years design cannot reuse the same UI or interaction model.

## Wave 1 — Two vertical slices

Build only one polished chapter of each. Do not generate hundreds of missions yet.

## Wave 2 — Technical platform extraction

Only after the slices work, extract reusable platform services that are genuinely shared:

- save/profile;
- achievements;
- accessibility settings;
- audio settings;
- curriculum mapping;
- secure grading;
- telemetry;
- teacher evidence.

Do **not** extract gameplay UI into one universal component.

## Wave 3 — Rebuild the strongest existing worlds

Suggested sequence:

1. CodeBots
2. Circuit Forge
3. GeoQuest
4. Reading Quest
5. Signal Shield
6. EcoGrid
7. BioQuest
8. Animation Story Lab

Then reassess remaining titles based on playtest evidence.

# Definition of portfolio success

The Arcade succeeds when a child can move from one title to another and immediately feel:

- a different role;
- different controls;
- different decision rhythm;
- different world rules;
- different visual composition;
- different audio identity;
- different type of mastery;

while still recognizing SukuuNova as the platform connecting those worlds.
