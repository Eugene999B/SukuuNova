# SukuuNova Learning Arcade — Studio Constitution

Status: **Pre-production law**  
Owner: Learning Arcade  
Applies to: every current and future SukuuNova game  

## Why this document exists

The Learning Arcade must never again be developed as one shared quiz application with different themes. A flagship earns the word **game** only when the learner's repeated actions, the world's rules, the feedback, the progression and the audiovisual experience combine into a distinct playable system.

This constitution is the release standard. A title that violates it does not ship as a flagship, regardless of how much curriculum content, UI polish or code already exists.

---

## 1. The five questions every game must answer before code

Every Game Bible must answer these in one sentence each:

1. **Who am I?** — the player's role/fantasy.
2. **What am I trying to accomplish?** — the visible goal in the world.
3. **What do I repeatedly do with my hands?** — the primary verbs and controls.
4. **What rules make those actions meaningful?** — the simulation/game system.
5. **Why do I want to return tomorrow?** — progression, mastery, story, creation, collection, competition or discovery.

If any answer is “read a question and choose one of four answers,” the design must be rejected unless that interaction is intrinsically the fantasy (for example, a game-show title such as Nova Millionaire).

---

## 2. A SukuuNova game is a system, not a worksheet

A game must contain:

- a player fantasy and coherent world;
- one or more primary verbs;
- rules that connect action to consequence;
- goals visible inside the fiction;
- meaningful choices, not fake buttons;
- feedback that makes cause and effect understandable;
- progression that changes capability, challenge, world state or expression;
- recovery after mistakes;
- replay value;
- a distinct audiovisual identity;
- assessment that is embedded in play where possible.

A lesson may contain a quiz. A flagship may contain occasional explicit questions. But the flagship itself cannot be a quiz shell.

---

## 3. The learning must live inside the mechanic

We use the **Learning-Action Test**:

> If the school subject were removed, would the player's action still make sense? If the subject is restored, does knowledge improve the player's ability to act?

Good examples:

- mathematics changes what the player can build, trade, measure, route or optimize;
- programming makes machines/robots/software actually execute;
- cybersecurity knowledge changes how the player investigates and protects a simulated system;
- geography changes route planning, map reading and spatial decisions;
- biology changes diagnosis, systems reasoning and interventions;
- history changes how evidence is ordered, compared and interpreted;
- language changes what can be constructed, communicated, decoded or persuaded;
- physics changes motion, force, balance, power or machinery.

Weak pattern to reject:

> Perform unrelated game animation → stop → answer school question → resume animation.

---

## 4. The Core Loop rule

Every title must define a 10–60 second **moment-to-moment loop** and a 5–30 minute **session loop**.

The moment-to-moment loop must contain at least three of:

- observe;
- choose a plan;
- manipulate/build/move/type/connect/trace/allocate/create;
- execute;
- inspect consequences;
- revise;
- collect/use resources;
- communicate with a character/system;
- discover something new.

The session loop must end with a meaningful change: a completed job, repaired system, expanded farm, unlocked district, improved robot, solved case, produced animation, earned licence/rank, completed chapter, or other persistent progress.

“Five questions completed” is not acceptable session progression.

---

## 5. Rules and fairness

A good game is learnable because its rules are consistent.

Every Game Bible must specify:

- success conditions;
- failure conditions;
- recovery rules;
- resource rules;
- scoring rules, if score exists;
- randomness rules;
- what information is visible before an action;
- what feedback appears after an action;
- what can and cannot be undone;
- how difficulty increases;
- how the game avoids unwinnable states.

Educational difficulty must come from the task and system, not from hidden punishment.

### Timing rule

Hard timers are allowed only when speed is the actual skill being learned or the fantasy genuinely requires time pressure. TurboType is the clear example. Reading, reasoning, engineering, coding, mathematics, geography, biology, history and similar thinking tasks must not damage state or reduce academic reward merely because the learner took longer.

---

## 6. Age architecture is not cosmetic difficulty

SukuuNova has four experience bands:

### Creche & KG — Play to discover

- low/no reading dependency;
- narration and sound-led instructions;
- large touch targets;
- tap, drag, trace, match, sort, speak/listen, simple movement;
- immediate cause-and-effect;
- short sessions and gentle repetition;
- no dense HUDs;
- no punishment loops.

### Primary — Adventure to understand

- exploration, building, collection, characters and short missions;
- concrete visual models before abstract notation;
- simple inventories/resources;
- short story arcs;
- creative expression;
- controls taught through play rather than manuals.

### JHS — Systems to master

- simulation, strategy, investigation, coding, resource management;
- linked systems with visible consequences;
- multi-step goals;
- career exposure;
- collaboration and project work where appropriate;
- increasing abstraction while keeping a strong world model.

### SHS — Practice to become

- authentic tools and professional simulations;
- realistic projects, briefs, constraints and trade-offs;
- deeper domain language;
- open-ended problem solving;
- career pathways;
- portfolio-quality outputs where possible;
- assessment based on process and product, not only selected answers.

A single screen with smaller/bigger numbers is not four age experiences.

---

## 7. Visual identity rule

The SukuuNova shell may share account, accessibility, parental/teacher reporting, achievements and navigation conventions.

Inside a flagship, the visual language belongs to that world.

A game must define its own:

- camera/presentation style;
- composition/grid rules;
- typography role;
- icon family;
- motion language;
- menu metaphor;
- HUD philosophy;
- interaction affordances;
- world map/level-select style;
- feedback animation;
- success/failure presentation;
- colour system;
- illustration/3D/2D direction;
- character direction.

Rejected pattern: same centred card, same top meters, same four buttons, same modal, same layout, recoloured per subject.

---

## 8. UX follows the fantasy

Interfaces should feel like tools from the world:

- farm management → fields, map, shed, market ledger;
- cybersecurity → SOC console, topology, inbox, logs, terminal;
- coding → blocks/IDE, simulator, debugger, terminal;
- engineering → workbench, schematic, instruments;
- medicine/biology → patient chart, body map, lab/scan interfaces;
- animation → timeline, stage, storyboard, layers;
- geography → maps, compass, route planner, field journal;
- business → storefront, inventory, till, invoices, city map;
- early learning → manipulable world objects, almost no conventional dashboard.

The UI can be usable and consistent without being visually identical.

---

## 9. Audio is gameplay, not decoration

Each title requires an Audio Bible covering:

- world ambience;
- interaction sounds;
- success/near-miss/failure feedback;
- music states;
- character/voice strategy;
- accessibility captions/visual equivalents;
- volume defaults;
- mute and independent music/SFX controls.

Audio must confirm actions and communicate state changes. Silent or barely audible generated tones do not count as an audiovisual identity.

---

## 10. Story is a progression engine

Not every game needs a dramatic plot, but every flagship must have a reason for the next mission to exist.

Possible structures:

- career growth;
- journey/adventure;
- city/world restoration;
- mystery/case campaign;
- tournament/season;
- business expansion;
- research programme;
- character/community relationships;
- creative studio career;
- exploration/discovery log.

Story must create context for mechanics. It must not be paragraphs pasted between identical rounds.

---

## 11. Persistence and return motivation

Every flagship must choose at least two return drivers:

- mastery/skill improvement;
- narrative progress;
- world growth;
- collection;
- customization;
- creation/portfolio;
- career rank/licences;
- social/cooperative goals;
- weekly challenges;
- discovery;
- strategic optimization.

Progression cannot rely only on XP, stars and badges.

---

## 12. Assessment and intelligence

SukuuNova may use curriculum metadata, procedural generation and AI-assisted systems, but intelligence must improve the game rather than expose the learner to uncontrolled content.

Rules:

- server-authoritative correctness for scored academic outcomes;
- no correct-answer leakage before commitment;
- deterministic/rubric-based checks for high-stakes outcomes where possible;
- generative content must pass age, grammar, ambiguity, safety and curriculum validation;
- creative taste is never graded as factual correctness;
- open-ended work uses transparent rubrics and/or teacher review;
- telemetry measures learning actions, misconceptions and strategy—not just click speed;
- adaptive difficulty changes the problem space, scaffolding and complexity, not only number size.

AI must not replace authored core mechanics or curriculum quality assurance.

---

## 13. Technology is chosen per game

No framework is mandatory across every title.

Approved architectural families include:

- React/DOM for tool-like simulations, management interfaces and accessible forms where appropriate;
- Phaser for 2D game loops, movement, scenes, sprites and arcade interactions;
- Canvas/WebGL/Pixi-style rendering for bespoke 2D worlds;
- Three.js-class 3D rendering when 3D materially improves the learning mechanic;
- robust physics libraries only where physical simulation matters;
- Blockly-style visual programming for beginner coding;
- Monaco-class code editing and terminal emulation for advanced programming experiences;
- Web Audio/Tone-style systems for music/rhythm/audio-rich titles.

The Architecture Review must justify every major dependency by player value, device cost, bundle cost, maintenance cost and accessibility impact.

---

## 14. Performance and school-device reality

A beautiful game that cannot run in schools is a failed educational product.

Every title defines:

- minimum target device class;
- mobile/touch support level;
- keyboard/mouse support level;
- offline/weak-network behaviour;
- asset budget;
- initial load budget;
- memory budget;
- reduced-motion mode;
- low-power mode where needed;
- graceful fallback for unsupported features.

Avoid importing a large library merely because it is fashionable.

---

## 15. Accessibility is designed, not patched

Each game must consider:

- keyboard access where appropriate;
- touch target sizes;
- colour-independent signals;
- reduced motion;
- captions/text equivalents for critical audio;
- readable type and contrast;
- dyslexia-friendly spacing options where applicable;
- narration for pre-readers;
- adjustable audio;
- no essential information conveyed only by transient animation;
- motor/cognitive load appropriate to age.

Accessibility options must preserve the learning goal rather than simply lowering all challenge.

---

## 16. Content scale and replay quality

Before release, a title must define:

- minimum authored mission count;
- procedural variation dimensions;
- repetition limits;
- narrative/scene variation;
- difficulty progression;
- curriculum coverage map;
- content QA ownership;
- stale-content detection.

A generator that changes names/numbers while repeating the same reasoning pattern does not count as meaningful variety.

---

## 17. Production gates

### Gate 0 — Concept

Requires: audience, fantasy, learning action, core verbs, differentiation from every other flagship.

### Gate 1 — Paper prototype

Requires: rules, state model, sample mission, failure/recovery, progression sketch.

### Gate 2 — Greybox fun prototype

Requires: playable core loop **without relying on polished art or a large question bank**.

Kill or redesign if the loop is not intrinsically understandable/engaging.

### Gate 3 — Learning prototype

Requires: curriculum concepts embedded into the mechanic; evidence that knowing more changes play quality.

### Gate 4 — Vertical slice

Requires: representative art, sound, UI, story, progression, accessibility and telemetry for one polished chapter/level.

### Gate 5 — Production

Requires: scalable content pipeline, performance budget, test plan, analytics schema and authored production backlog.

### Gate 6 — Release candidate

Requires: child playtesting, teacher/curriculum review, accessibility QA, security review, content QA and full CI.

A title may not jump from Concept directly to “hundreds of generated questions.”

---

## 18. Playtest questions

For every observed session, the team must answer:

- Did the learner understand what to do without a lecture?
- What did their hands do most often?
- Did the world visibly react to their decisions?
- Was knowledge useful to play better?
- Did they voluntarily explore or experiment?
- Did they understand mistakes?
- Did they recover from mistakes?
- Did they ask to continue?
- Could they describe what made this game different from another SukuuNova game?
- Did the UI look like this game's world, or like the generic platform shell?

If the team cannot answer the last two positively, the flagship is not ready.

---

## 19. Portfolio rule

SukuuNova does not need 100 shallow games. It needs a smaller number of worlds with enough mechanical and narrative depth to support years of growth.

Before adding a new flagship, ask whether the concept should instead become:

- a new campaign inside an existing world;
- a career path;
- an age-band evolution;
- an expansion;
- a new district/biome;
- a creative tool;
- a multiplayer/co-op mode.

Depth beats catalogue count.

---

## 20. Final shipping sentence

Before release, the team must be able to complete this statement:

> **In this game, the learner is a ______ who repeatedly ______ in order to ______. The world responds by ______. Learning ______ makes them measurably better at the game. They return because ______. This experience could not be mistaken for any other SukuuNova flagship because ______.**

If the sentence is weak, the game is not ready to build.
