# Ghanaian nursing and language learning update

## Programme boundaries
General Nursing retains the RGN diploma (three years) and BSc (four years) paths. Registered Midwifery has a distinct three-year diploma map. Paediatric Nursing is labelled specialist/post-basic study, not an entry-level diploma. The specialist stages are study groupings, not a claim about every institution's duration. Course menus do not split by semester.

The supplied Kumasi RM 24 timetable is explicitly Registered Midwifery, Year 1, Semester 1, September–December 2026. Its seven subjects are anatomy and physiology, basic nursing, microbiology, therapeutic communication, professional adjustment, nursing informatics and first aid. Basic Nursing is represented by the existing Fundamentals of Nursing course to preserve that course's identity. The timetable is evidence of those first-semester subjects only. Other courses were mapped from published Ghanaian college lists, not inferred from the timetable.

## Original foundation content
36 original concepts support four task formats: concept identification, a supervised scenario, a typed term and review of a documented response. These cover communication, professionalism, informatics, first aid, microbiology and introductory child assessment/family care. This is 144 task presentations, not 144 independently reviewed clinical competencies and not millions of authored questions. Stable exposure keys survive seed and answer-order changes.

Content is original practice, not reproduced official examination material. Clinical educators must review content before it is described as clinically validated or suitable for high-stakes assessment. Text practice does not certify clinical skills.

## Language and answer changes
Replace the fabricated 48-million-per-topic language count with bounded authored-task counts. Remove seed-dependent identities and grammar selection lockstep. Add typed grammar responses and distinct upper-level French/Twi passages with evidence and inference questions. Preserve Unicode accents and Ghanaian letters during answer normalisation.

Always show a readable correct answer and a separate explanation after submission. Multiple-choice answers display their labels, never an internal option number. Blank numeric responses cannot be marked as zero.

Remaining content work includes larger independently reviewed banks, more Ghanaian languages, composition rubrics with human review, validated listening recordings, advanced clinical curricula, and subject-by-subject depth audits. These changes do not claim that all basic-to-university content is complete.
